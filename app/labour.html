Labour

Unemployment at 2.5% — among the lowest in the developed world.

Japan's unemployment rate has stayed below 3.5% for the whole decade, peaking at 3.4% in 2015 and easing to 2.5% in 2024. The labour market's binding constraint is not jobs — it is workers.

What the rate means

Japan's unemployment rate is low by any standard: the OECD average is roughly twice as high. Two forces hold it down — a shrinking working-age population and a labour market where firms hoard workers rather than shed them in a downturn.

The pandemic's effect was small and brief: unemployment rose from 2.4% in 2019 to 2.8% in 2020 and was back to 2.6% by 2022.

What to watch instead

With unemployment this low, the telling indicators are elsewhere: the size of the labour force, the share of non-regular work, and wages. The spring wage round (shunto) and the Statistics Bureau's Labour Force Survey are the releases to follow.

See Population for the shrinking working-age population behind the tight labour market, and Prices for what wages buy.
