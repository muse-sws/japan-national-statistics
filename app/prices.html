Prices

After decades near zero, prices rose 2.7% in 2024.

From 2015 to 2021 consumer inflation in Japan averaged about 0.3% a year — twice dipping below zero. In 2022 it jumped to 2.5%, then 3.3% in 2023, the fastest since the early 1990s. In 2024 it eased to 2.7%.

Source: World Bank World Development Indicators (FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG), accessed 26 November 2026

Why 2022 changed everything

Japan spent a generation fighting deflation. The Bank of Japan's 2% target, set in 2013, was missed for nine straight years. Then energy and food prices surged in 2022, the yen fell, and inflation arrived from abroad — not from domestic demand.

That matters for what comes next: inflation driven by import prices squeezes households unless wages rise with it.

What to watch

The Bank of Japan's core-core CPI (excluding fresh food and energy), the spring wage round, and the yen. If wages outpace prices, the inflation of 2022–2024 becomes a normal cycle; if not, it is a pay cut in disguise.
