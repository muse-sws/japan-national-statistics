Regions

Half the country lives in eight prefectures.

Japan has 47 prefectures, but its people are concentrated: the eight largest hold 63,984,000 people — 50.7% of the 2020 census total. The Tokyo metropolitan area alone (Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama and Chiba) holds 36,914,000, about 30% of the country.

Source: Statistics Bureau of Japan, 2020 Population Census

Concentration is rising

Between the 2015 and 2020 censuses the population grew in only 8 of the 47 prefectures — Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama, Chiba, Aichi, Fukuoka, Okinawa and Shiga — and fell in the other 39. Tokyo grew fastest, by 3.9%.

The smallest prefecture, Tottori, counted about 550,000 people in 2020 — one twenty-fifth of Tokyo.

Three metropolitan areas

Most Japanese live in one of three metropolitan areas: Greater Tokyo (about 37 million), Keihanshin around Osaka and Kyoto, and Chukyo around Nagoya in Aichi. The rest of the country — 39 prefectures — shares the other half of the population.
